# 13. Agentic RAG & Advanced Retrieval

AegisOps is asked, "Why did checkout latency rise after Tuesday's release, and have we seen this before?" The answer may require metrics, deployment history, runbooks and earlier incidents. One fixed query may not establish the necessary connections. Agentic RAG gives a model bounded control over whether to retrieve, which sources to inspect, how to reformulate questions and when the collected evidence is sufficient. The source and access discipline from Chapter 12 remains unchanged.

This chapter makes that adaptive research process explicit. We will plan subquestions, route them to appropriate sources and retain one evidence ledger. We will also limit repeated searches, distinguish association from explanation and govern structured queries. The goal is a supported answer with a traceable research path, not the appearance of diligence produced by many expensive lookups.

:::analogy A reporter with an editor and a notebook
A reporter investigating a story decides who to interview next based on the last answer. The editor asks whether the important questions are covered, whether sources conflict and whether the story can be published. A notebook preserves who said what and when. A deadline prevents endless calls that add nothing. Where it stops: counting two sources does not prove independence or truth. Software needs source authority, access checks, claim support and explicit uncertainty; several copied articles can repeat the same unsupported assertion.
:::

## What makes RAG agentic

A fixed RAG pipeline can already rewrite a question, search several indexes, rerank candidates and verify citations. It becomes agentic when the model adaptively controls retrieval decisions from observations rather than merely following authored stages. The difference is control, not sophistication. A deterministic router can choose a source using known rules, while a model-directed investigation can switch sources after discovering a missing deployment record.

Skip retrieval only under a stated contract. A greeting needs no policy search, while an order's current refundable balance requires authoritative lookup. Facts retained in state may be sufficient when their freshness and scope fit the decision. Do not use model confidence alone to decide that private or current evidence is unnecessary. The retrieval choice should be visible in the trajectory and evaluated for missing-source errors as well as cost savings.

## Retrieval routing and source selection

Give each retrieval capability a distinct purpose. search_policies answers eligibility rules; search_runbooks finds procedures; search_incidents finds earlier episodes; query_metrics supplies governed time series; query_orders returns permitted aggregates; graph_neighbors supplies relationships. Descriptions should name coverage, effective-date behavior and expected evidence. A generic search_everything often hides authority and makes wrong-source citations easier.

A cheap structured router can propose sources for each subquestion. The investigator may override that proposal within the allowed capability set when new evidence justifies a change. Evaluate the router separately: does it direct quantitative questions to metrics rather than an old incident paragraph? Does it distinguish customer policy from an operator's runbook? Include escape and missing-source outcomes, and preserve the user's identity and entitlement through every downstream query.

Source availability changes what can be claimed. If metrics are inaccessible, a document saying "latency increased" may be historical context but cannot substitute for the requested quantitative current measurement. Record the source gap in the evidence-sufficiency result. A useful answer can explain what was established, what remains unknown and what access or record would resolve it, rather than laundering weaker evidence into a confident number.

## Query rewriting, decomposition and multi-query search

Rewriting makes a request explicit, resolving references such as "it" to the named service and expressing the time window. Retain the original question so the rewritten query can be checked for changed meaning. Adding an unsupported assumption, such as "connection-pool exhaustion," can bias retrieval before any evidence establishes it. A rewrite should improve discoverability while preserving uncertainty and the actual task.

Decomposition splits a compound question into independently answerable parts. For AegisOps, determine when latency changed, which release/configuration changed near that time and whether comparable earlier incidents exist. Route those parts to metrics, change records and incident sources. The final synthesis must reconnect them: temporal proximity is evidence of association, not proof that the release caused the symptom. Hypotheses need additional checks against alternatives and the system's current behavior.

Multi-query retrieval uses several phrasings and fuses their source identifiers. Count each actual backend query, cap fanout and deduplicate passages by stable source/version identity. Hypothetical-document retrieval, often called HyDE, generates an imagined answer-style document to improve embedding retrieval when questions and passages differ in wording. That hypothetical text is a search aid, not evidence. Only retrieved verified sources may support the published conclusion.

## Iterative retrieval and evidence sufficiency

Define sufficiency for the question before the search expands. Every required subquestion needs appropriate relevant evidence, and contradictions affecting the conclusion must be resolved or disclosed. A structured check can return covered questions with evidence identifiers, unresolved gaps, conflicts and a proposed next action. Validate the identifiers against the ledger and use deterministic coverage rules alongside model judgment. A field saying covered without any supporting record is not meaningful verification.

The controller bounds rounds, actual source queries, latency and total cost. It remembers normalized queries to prevent unchanged repetition and records why a follow-up adds value. Repeated queries may occasionally be legitimate after a source update, but that needs a freshness/wait contract rather than an immediate loop. Keep budget outcomes distinct from sufficient evidence: reaching a limit is not a reason to generate the answer as if research succeeded.

Detect diminishing returns using new accepted evidence, not list length. Appending the same three passages again grows a list while adding no information. Maintain a ledger keyed by source, immutable version and chunk/record identity. A changed source version may be new; a repeated summary of the same paragraph is not an independent source. Score novelty and relevance under the task so unrelated new passages also fail to justify continued work.

```python
def add_evidence(ledger, candidates):
    added = []
    for item in candidates:
        key = (item["source"], item["version"], item["locator"])
        if not item["permitted"] or not item["relevant"]:
            continue
        if key in ledger:
            continue
        ledger[key] = item
        added.append(item["id"])
    return added
```

The complete function assumes upstream metadata validation and a declared relevance decision. A production ledger must also validate stable identifier uniqueness, conflict updates and source authority. Its returned added list gives the controller an observable progress signal. Test a round returning only earlier records: it should add nothing and trigger the chosen stop/alternative-source behavior. Hard round limits still matter even when novelty detection works.

## Corrective retrieval and recovery from weak evidence

Corrective retrieval assesses candidates before relying on them. If most are irrelevant, rewrite the question, choose another authorized source or adjust permitted relevance filters. If the corpus cannot answer it, use an approved fallback such as public web search for suitable public information, or report insufficiency. Do not broaden tenant, user entitlement or provider-egress restrictions in the name of recovery. Those are authority boundaries, not search-tuning parameters.

A relevance grader can be a reranker, a small model or a domain rule. Its decision should be inspectable and evaluated. "Partially relevant" may mean a passage supplies one subquestion but not the whole answer; preserve that distinction rather than dropping it as useless or accepting it as complete. Search failure may also be a content, freshness or permission issue. More query variations will not repair a missing authoritative record.

## Self-RAG and critique-oriented patterns

Self-RAG is a research approach that trains models to generate retrieval and critique-related signals. A practical application can approximate some of its behaviors using structured decisions before retrieval and support checks after drafting. That approximation is not the same training method or a promise of the same measured results. Describe which checks are actually implemented: retrieve-or-not, passage relevance, per-claim support and usefulness against the original question.

As in Chapter 3, critique earns its cost by checking something concrete. A post-draft support checker opens cited passages and distinguishes accepted, unsupported and uncertain claims. It can trigger targeted retrieval for a missing part or remove an unsupported conclusion. Its own errors need evaluation and spot review. A polished self-score without external evidence is not a reliable answer-release gate for consequential decisions.

## Graph RAG and knowledge-graph retrieval

Relationship questions need more than topical similarity. To ask which customer-facing services depend on payments-db, traverse an authoritative dependency graph with service and edge metadata. Retain the path and its source/version as evidence, and enforce entity/edge access rules. Stale topology can produce a confident wrong blast radius, so graph freshness belongs to the same information contract as document freshness.

Graph-enhanced corpus retrieval is another approach. Entity relationships and community summaries can support broad questions about themes across incident reports that no single passage answers. Microsoft GraphRAG is a concrete implementation in this area; it should not be confused with every use of a graph database. Extraction and summary errors propagate into relationships and global answers, so retain source lineage and evaluate the questions the graph representation is intended to improve.

Choose the simpler representation when it suffices. A governed service catalog may answer ownership questions without an LLM-extracted graph. A document index may answer a specific policy clause more directly than community summaries. Graph construction adds maintenance, cost and access-control obligations. It is useful when relationship traversal or global synthesis produces measurable value, not merely because multi-hop sounds more advanced than retrieval.

## SQL and structured data as governed knowledge tools

Many questions need exact counts, amounts or trends. Prefer a narrow typed query operation or curated semantic layer when the required aggregates are known. Let the model select parameters such as service, date range and grouping, while reviewed SQL determines the query. Use a database role limited to appropriate views and row-level security for the actual principal. A read query can still leak data or consume substantial resources, so enforce row and statement-time limits.

If free-form text-to-SQL is required, treat generated SQL as untrusted input. Parse it under the chosen dialect, require a single permitted statement and inspect the entire syntax tree for writes, locking, forbidden functions and destinations. Resolve tables and CTE references by scope and schema. A global set of CTE names cannot justify skipping every physical table with the same name. Reject unsupported constructs explicitly rather than claiming a short parser walk is a complete safety boundary.

Allowlist columns as well as tables when the contract promises field minimization. Validate qualified names and reject or deliberately normalize nonliteral/negative/oversized limits. LIMIT controls returned rows, not necessarily database effort: aggregates, joins and scans may still be expensive. A read-only role, restricted functions, row security, statement_timeout and resource controls provide the actual containment. AST validation is additional defense and needs malicious-query tests against pinned parser and database versions.

The following query function illustrates a constrained operation rather than an unrestricted SQL agent. It expects an authorized, appropriately configured PostgreSQL connection and a reviewed view with row-level policy. Its explicit field projection and parameter binding keep model values out of SQL syntax. The connection's role, transaction and timeout are caller-owned contracts; the function does not pretend to authenticate the user or create row-level security by itself.

```python
def recent_orders(connection, tenant, since, limit=100):
    if type(limit) is not int or not 1 <= limit <= 500:
        raise ValueError("limit must be between 1 and 500")
    sql = """
        SELECT order_id, status, total_minor, created_at
        FROM analytics.orders_v
        WHERE tenant_id = %s AND created_at >= %s
        ORDER BY created_at DESC
        LIMIT %s
    """
    with connection.cursor() as cursor:
        cursor.execute(sql, (tenant, since, limit))
        return cursor.fetchall()
```

## Keeping evidence traceable across sources

Normalize documents, metrics, SQL results, graphs and incident records into one ledger. Each entry has a stable evidence identifier, source type, locator, selected content, retrieval/effective times, version and authority/access metadata. For SQL, retain the reviewed query identity or normalized query and parameter context under privacy policy. For metrics, retain the series query and time window. A summary alone cannot support later audit if its underlying record is gone.

Every published factual claim references ledger IDs whose locators a permitted reader can inspect. The final synthesis should preserve disagreement and distinguish observed numbers from hypotheses. If refunds rose after a policy change, state the measured association and date; do not assert the change caused the increase without further evidence. An evidence ledger makes that discipline practical across several tools, while citation support checking establishes whether the conclusion actually follows.

## Production lens

An adaptive research system has source-specific contracts, explicit sufficiency and deterministic limits. Queries and accepted unique evidence are observable, weak retrieval has a corrective path and unauthorized filters never broaden. SQL and graph access stay governed, and every claim retains source lineage. Track success, support, actual query count, latency and total cost. Adaptive control should produce better supported outcomes than the fixed baseline, not simply a longer trace.

## Failure modes

The loop can repeat searches, answer prematurely, cite the wrong source type, launder summaries into evidence or use unsafe structured queries. Stale graphs can misstate impact, and missing-source workers can produce incomplete synthesis. Diagnose by the ledger and control decisions: what was queried, what unique evidence was accepted and which gap supposedly became covered. These records explain whether the repair belongs to routing, retrieval quality, source governance or release verification.

## Interview drills

Describe why a question needs adaptive research, then identify its subquestions and source authorities. Explain the sufficiency rule and how no-new-evidence differs from merely receiving another response. For SQL, describe database containment before parser validation. For graph retrieval, name the relationships and their freshness source. For synthesis, distinguish observed association from a causal claim. The strongest answer traces a failed research path as clearly as a successful one.

## Key takeaways

Agentic RAG adds bounded adaptive control to a quality-controlled retrieval foundation. It plans gaps, queries appropriate sources and stops according to coverage, support and resource limits. Unique attributed records become evidence; hypothetical search text and repeated passages do not. Graphs and structured queries extend what can be known while adding authority and maintenance obligations. The final answer should explain both the supported conclusion and the limits of the research.
