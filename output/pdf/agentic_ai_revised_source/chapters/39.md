# 39. More Capstone Projects

AegisOps connects the whole discipline, but a large unfinished system is weak evidence of engineering ability. A focused project can prove one difficult boundary more clearly: financial effects under failure, answers under insufficient evidence, or code changes under an independent acceptance process. This chapter offers three complete tracks. Each begins with a user need, defines the risky outcome, grows through testable milestones and finishes with a failure demonstration a reviewer can reproduce.

Choose the capstone track and scope before tooling. A simulated track with fakes, fixtures and a clear README can be completed far sooner than live enterprise integration. The original planning ranges of one to two weeks for refunds, two to three for research and two to four for coding assume a narrow task and available scaffolding. They are not delivery commitments. Record your own experience, dependencies and operating assumptions rather than treating calendar estimates as evidence of completeness.

:::analogy Preparing a practical case for an examination board
A trainee chooses a case that demonstrates the skills being assessed, writes down the procedure and evidence, then explains a complication and the response. A bigger case is not automatically better; an incomplete case may show less judgment than a smaller one carefully finished. Your capstone similarly needs inspectable work, not just an attractive story. Where it stops: passing a portfolio review does not license an agent to handle real money, confidential decisions or production code without organizational approval and operating safeguards.
:::

## Choosing a track

Track A suits backend or product engineering focused on accountable actions. Track B suits data, retrieval and analytical work focused on evidence. Track C suits developer tooling focused on isolated execution and resumable tasks. AegisOps suits incident-response and platform work across several systems. Choose what your target role values, then identify the evidence that will establish it. Do not choose the track with the greatest number of agents merely because its architecture looks ambitious.

Two focused tracks can show more range than half of a broad platform. A refund project demonstrates authority and effect recovery; a research project demonstrates meaningful refusal and source reasoning. Their shared harness can reduce duplicated work while their verifiers remain distinct. Keep both complete: unfinished evaluation and recovery cannot be excused by the fact that the repository contains two demos. A capstone's useful size is the scope you can test and explain honestly.

## Track A - ShopAssist Refunds

### The brief and people

Build Northwind's support agent for order questions and duplicate-capture refunds. It reads current order and payment facts, retrieves the applicable policy, explains eligibility, proposes a precise remedy, obtains required authority and dispatches a recoverable refund. The financial goal is one intended effect under the provider's documented contract, not a claim that local database uniqueness makes every remote operation exactly once.

The customer wants a clear explanation grounded in policy and order facts. The support lead wants an approval card that makes the payment, amount and justification visible. The finance controller wants reconciliation linking each effect to its authority and accounting. These stories become acceptance checks: every eligibility statement identifies its sources, every proposal has complete evidence, and failure injection cannot create another refund for the same logical effect.

### Architecture and records

One bounded investigator uses get-order, list-payments and search-policy tools. A deterministic eligibility service verifies ownership, applicable policy and current captured/refunded balance. A proposal includes payment ID, order, amount in integer minor units, currency, canonical hash, evidence and logical effect identity. The approval service checks the permitted support role and stores expiry and consumption. A narrow executor exchanges authority for a token accepted by the payment service.

Conversation records own evidence and proposals. Approval refers to one immutable proposal; execution records pending, applied, rejected or unknown outcome. Uniqueness prevents duplicate local intents, while payment-level accounting prevents distinct proposals from exceeding the refundable amount. Provider idempotency or reconciliation closes the external crash window. Document key-retention limits: a provider that forgets a key after a day cannot be treated as preserving it forever. The [Stripe idempotency reference](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests) illustrates that distinction.

The executor reserves or consumes approval according to a recoverable transaction protocol, persists intent, and calls the provider. If the response is lost, continuation queries or reuses the same retained key rather than issuing a new refund. An immediate approval replay is denied by consumption and action identity even before expiry. A stale balance or changed payment state blocks execution. Matching hashes establish arguments, not current eligibility or single use.

### Milestones

A1 answers fifty labeled order and policy questions with source support and at least ninety-percent correctness. A2 creates eligibility proposals only when order, payment and policy evidence are available. A3 introduces a durable approval wait, narrow execution and provider uncertainty; kill the worker during approval and after provider processing. A4 adds an eval gate, injection cases, reconciliation and operating visibility. Every milestone stays runnable with fakes before real credentials are introduced.

At A3, the fake payments service must model the difficult boundary: it can process the refund and then raise a timeout. Persist its effect ledger across processes. A local worker counter cannot prove the financial effect after restart. Also create two different runs that try to refund the same payment: stable business accounting and effect identity must prevent over-refund, not merely deduplicate identical transport requests. This case distinguishes useful financial correctness from a narrow retry demo.

### Evals and release gates

Use chaos testing for crash and timing faults, alongside one hundred and twenty labeled conversations with success at least ninety percent and a two-sided 95% Wilson lower bound at least eighty-five percent. Under that interval definition, 108 passes give a lower bound around 83.3%, while 110 passes give about 85.3%; both requirements must hold. Specify the independent case unit and repeated-trial aggregation. Include no-proposal-without-evidence, zero duplicate logical effects and at least forty injected note/email cases with no successful forbidden action.

Measure policy citation accuracy, approval precision, reconciliation correctness and cost per successful conversation. An illustrative median model-cost target is three cents, to be recalculated for actual prompts and pricing. Live use needs a sufficiently broad, risk-appropriate corpus and operational review, not just these curriculum numbers. A high overall score cannot offset an over-refund, unauthorized action or unresolved outcome presented as success.

### Stretch goals and what to show

Add partial refunds only with cumulative accounting and explicit amount/currency rules. Add a second language or voice channel while retaining the same proposal binding. An autonomy increase for a small duplicate-capture segment needs error/reversal evidence, reviewed policy and bounded rollout; a high approval rate alone can reflect rubber-stamping. Keep a route back to supervised execution when quality or operating conditions change.

The demo kills a worker after the provider applies a refund but before local completion, then resumes to show one effect and linked audit. Replay the still-unexpired approval and show denial by consumed authority, not by inventing a hash mismatch. Next change the current refundable balance and show stale execution blocked. A two-minute failure sequence explains more than several transcripts whose final sentences merely say the same refund happened.

## Track B - Policy Research Analyst

### The brief and people

Build an analyst answering questions that combine policy documents with governed operational data, such as whether refund rates rose after a returns-policy change. The agent decomposes the question, chooses sources, records evidence, resolves stated source precedence and writes only supported claims. Its difficult outcome is a confident wrong answer. It has no production mutation tool, but confidentiality, outbound access and misleading decisions remain real risks.

The analyst wants reusable numbers and quotations with sources. The operations manager wants gaps exposed before making a decision. The compliance reviewer wants conflicting dates or definitions recorded with the rule used to resolve them. Acceptance therefore requires every number to trace to a query and population, every quotation to a versioned document, and every conflict to remain visible. Correct refusal is measured separately from correctness on answerable questions.

### Architecture and data

A planner creates subquestions and routes them to policy retrieval, allowlisted read-only SQL and the policy register. An evidence ledger stores source/version, query reference, observed time, freshness and supporting content. Answer claims contain their own evidence-ID lists. Conflict records link the disagreeing entries, applied rule and selected or unresolved result. The writer receives only sufficient governed evidence and cannot fill missing dimensions with general model knowledge.

For the worked question, a FAQ says the new returns policy began March 15 while the governing register says March 1. The stated system-of-record rule selects March 1 and records the disagreement. Query comparable populations for eight weeks before and after that date using an explicit definition of refund rate. Report 3.6% versus 4.4% as an illustrative result, with numerator and denominator evidence; do not infer causation from a before/after difference alone.

### The refusal that remains factual

Suppose the user asks for Q3 refund rate among marketplace sellers, but the permitted view has no seller-type dimension. The agent can say that the available data does not support the requested breakdown. It cannot conclude that marketplace orders are excluded unless the view definition explicitly establishes that fact. Nor can it add a first-party rate without a query that actually returned it. A careful refusal must be grounded as strictly as a positive answer.

The answer names what was checked, which missing dimension matters and where the gap was recorded for the data owner. If a supported partial figure exists, show its exact population and evidence separately. Say “no applicable policy was found in the approved sources checked,” not “no policy exists.” This language preserves the limit of the investigation while still helping the analyst decide what data or definition to request next.

### Milestones and gates

B1 ingests, retrieves and cites the corpus, measuring recall@10 on sixty labeled questions. B2 adds read-only SQL with database permissions, full-query validation, row bounds and time/cost limits; eighty reference questions measure execution accuracy. B3 adds planning, ledger, conflicts and sufficiency checks. B4 adds the unanswerable set, groundedness evaluation and dashboards. Validate joins, units and policy versions, not only SQL syntax or the presence of a citation marker.

Illustrative gates are ninety-five-percent supported claims, ninety-percent SQL execution accuracy, ninety-percent correct refusals on forty unanswerable cases and at most two-percent confident wrong answers under a defined rubric. Set latency and cost targets, such as p95 within twenty seconds and median model spend within fifteen cents, from the measured workload. Test source outage, stale policy, missing period and a forbidden tenant. An agent that refuses everything cannot pass the answerable-set gate.

### Stretch goals and demonstration

Graph retrieval can follow policy relationships when reliable entity data exists. Freshness notifications can identify owners of obsolete sources under an authorized communication policy. Analyst corrections can become reviewed evaluation cases without automatically becoming procedural truth. The project should remain read-only unless a separately designed effect product is added; “helpfulness” is not sufficient reason to grant policy or database updates.

Start the demonstration with the unsupported marketplace question and its accurate gap statement. Then show the date conflict, its governing rule, the query and the limited before/after conclusion. Finally remove one source and show degraded confidence or a partial answer. A reviewer should be able to click from each claim to its ledger evidence. The visible narrative is valuable because the data model supports inspection, not because it contains many citations.

## Track C - Repository Maintenance Agent

### The brief and people

Build an agent that turns narrowly scoped maintenance tickets into reviewable pull requests. Start with seeded bugs or flaky behavior in production code, not arbitrary feature requests. The maintainer wants less repetitive work; the reviewer wants small diffs with actual tests and attempted approaches; the platform owner wants isolation and independent acceptance. The agent operates through a disposable sandbox and cannot merge its own changes.

Protect tests, CI, secrets and infrastructure under the default repair mode. A dependency-upgrade mode is separate and reviewed: it can change declared manifests and lockfiles while retaining protected CI and verification. Without that distinction, advertising dependency upgrades while forbidding their necessary lockfile changes is contradictory. The policy should name allowed paths and expected artifact changes for each task mode before the model receives the ticket.

### Architecture and boundaries

The harness creates an isolated repository checkout with limited files, resources and network. It supplies approved instructions from a trusted ref and a bounded editor/command interface. The candidate code, repository scripts and tests remain untrusted execution. Secrets management stays outside candidate execution. Package access uses a controlled mirror without exposing credentials to the agent. A harness-owned diff guard compares final changes against the trusted base, including renamed paths and links that could bypass simple glob checks.

The hidden-test definitions stay out of the editable sandbox, but their execution against candidate code occurs in another equally isolated environment without host or harness secrets. Passing that set is evidence of behavior on held-out cases, not proof against all attacks. The PR writer lives outside the candidate environment and exposes only the permitted publication action. Git authentication belongs there, scoped to the operation, rather than mounted into the test runner.

### Work across sessions

The progress file records ticket, acceptance criteria, plan, learned facts, attempts, current branch/commit, check evidence, next step and open questions. Keep it versioned with the work and bounded in size. On resume, read it and reconcile actual files, branch and external artifacts. It can guide orientation but cannot prove that a command or PR succeeded. A prior crash can leave the file behind the actual state.

For a flaky payment test, session one reproduces failures and tries weakening an assertion; the guard denies the protected edit and records the attempt. Session two starts with a fresh context, reads the rejected idea and investigates the ledger-write ordering. It repairs the production behavior or an allowed interface, then runs repeated reproductions and the full suite. If the legitimate repair requires a protected test change, escalate for reviewed scope instead of evading the guard.

### Milestones and evaluation

C1 maps the repository and answers thirty questions with verified file/line evidence. C2 fixes at least seven of ten seeded bugs under sandbox and path policy, with no forbidden edits. C3 resumes one task across three sessions, reconciling progress without repeating a known failed attempt blindly. C4 evaluates thirty held-out tickets using hidden acceptance and explicit time/cost budgets. Keep benchmark tasks separate from the agent's examples and progress templates.

Illustrative gates include sixty-percent hidden-task success, zero successful protected-path, secret or egress violations, complete test/attempt/rationale evidence, and at least fifty-percent reviewer acceptance without rework. A median model-spend target of $1.50 per ticket excludes sandbox and human review unless the report explicitly adds them. Thirty tickets are a learning benchmark, not proof across arbitrary repositories. Report task slices, repeated instability and failures alongside successes.

### Stretch goals and demonstration

A reviewed dependency-upgrade campaign can operate across repositories under its separate path mode. A critic may inspect diff and evidence while the independent checks retain control. Repository-specific knowledge can become owned skills. Flaky-test clustering can prioritize work, but its similarity score should not justify applying the same fix without reproducing each case. Keep changes small enough for a human to understand and reverse.

Show the failed shortcut, its deterministic rejection and the real fix. Reproduce the issue, inspect the final diff, link actual command results and show continuation across sessions. A green suite without a believable acceptance contract can be gamed; a detailed PR body without actual checks is only a story. The demonstration should let a reviewer distinguish both defects and see why this run satisfies the particular ticket.

## The architecture pack for each track

Every track needs five complementary views: parts and owners, one critical runtime flow, owned records, authority boundaries and a visible failure. Track A shows bound refund authority and external effect recovery. Track B shows claims, source precedence and an evidence gap. Track C shows isolated candidate execution, protected paths and cross-session reconciliation. The diagrams should answer different questions rather than rename the same row of boxes.

Add the artifacts not visible in the drawings: approval card and payment threat model, source ownership/freshness map, or repair-mode path and egress policy. Keep diagrams aligned with actual interfaces. An arrow marked “approved” should point to a record and validator, not only a message. A record marked “verified” should name the check that established it. This is what makes an architecture pack useful for review rather than ornamental documentation.

## One rubric for every track

Use Chapter 38's portfolio weights with track-specific evidence. Safety in refunds means bound authority and correct accounting under failure; in research it means entitlement and supported limits; in coding it means isolated execution and independent path/check enforcement. Reliability means crash recovery, source-outage handling or verified cross-session continuation respectively. Hard safety gates remain separate from weighted scores, so strong writing cannot compensate for an unauthorized effect.

The rubric is a learning and communication tool. Apply it with a peer and ask for reproducible evidence for each score. Label simulated integrations and unresolved assumptions. A project that explains its limits clearly can show strong engineering judgment without claiming production certification. Reviewers need to see what was established, which failure modes were covered and what approval or operating work remains before live use.

## Failure modes

Across tracks, evaluation leakage can make success look better than it is; keep held-out cases separate and version them. Track A can approve stale accounting or duplicate an effect through another proposal, requiring current business constraints and reconciliation. Track B can turn missing fields into invented source coverage, requiring strict claim support. Track C can pass by weakening tests or exploit the judge, requiring harness-owned policy and isolation. Each failure should become a specific negative case.

Do not hide unfinished work behind a demo-only environment. Milestones must run repeatedly, and crash/uncertainty cases must inspect external records. If the benchmark exposes a limitation, record it rather than adjusting the grader to match the model. The practical examination analogy rewards a well-understood complication: the boundary where the agent stops or asks for a scoped change often demonstrates more judgment than another easy success.

## Interview drills

For platform engineering, choose coding when the role centers on developer tooling or AegisOps when it centers on operations. Explain the relevant evidence rather than declaring one project universally stronger. For research refusal, describe both an unanswerable set and the answerable-set false-refusal rate. For hidden tests, explain independent acceptance and isolated execution. For refunds, show the provider-side crash window and immediate authority replay, not just a repeated chat transcript.

## Key takeaways

Pick the proof, then build the smallest complete track around it. Stories become tests, records support inspection, milestones preserve working scope and negative cases expose the difficult boundary. The three tracks share runtime discipline while requiring distinct verification. A finished capstone makes its evidence and limitations easy to understand; the reviewer should never have to infer financial correctness, groundedness or sandbox safety from the model's confidence.
