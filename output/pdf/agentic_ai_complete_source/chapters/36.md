# 36. The AegisOps Blueprint

AegisOps is an incident commander for software services. It gathers observations, develops competing explanations, proposes remediation and coordinates approved action. Its goal is verified recovery or an honest safe escalation, with evidence that a human can inspect. This is an ambitious capstone because it joins the book's mechanisms in one coherent task. The blueprint describes a production-oriented design; completing a simulated portfolio does not certify a live production deployment.

We will follow a bad release that increases latency and errors in order-api. AegisOps must establish which version changed, whether the symptoms are actually connected to it, and whether rollback is permitted. The incident provides a concrete thread through identity, evidence, durable execution, approval, cost and verification. Alternative causes and unavailable dependencies are part of the design, not extensions added after the happy path works.

:::analogy The emergency-room team
A patient arrives with symptoms, not a proven diagnosis. A coordinating physician asks specialists for evidence, considers alternatives, obtains appropriate consent, treats and observes recovery. AegisOps follows that coordination pattern for software incidents: triage, evidence, hypothesis, approved action and observation. Where it stops: an AI incident commander is not a clinician, and medical consent rules do not define software authority. Our permissions, action classes and verification criteria are explicit engineering policy enforced outside model judgment.
:::

## The business scenario

The enterprise operates services on Kubernetes with deployments managed through reviewed change systems. Alerts, logs, traces and change records arrive from different owners. A new order-api release is followed by elevated p95 latency and 5xx responses. Human responders must connect time windows, versions and dependency behavior before deciding what to do. AegisOps shortens that evidence-gathering work while keeping high-impact production changes under deterministic authority.

The supported journey begins with an owned alert, proceeds through scoped investigation, and ends in verified resolution or escalation. Communication and learning follow recorded facts. Every transition has a reason, and every effect has a recoverable record. If AegisOps is unavailable, the organization's ordinary alerting still reaches people. The agent is an assistant to incident response, not a single point of failure replacing the team's ability to respond.

## Who AegisOps serves: personas and user stories

Asha, the on-call SRE, wants a useful evidence summary quickly and fears an unexplained restart. Marco, the incident manager, wants one timeline and fears contradictory updates. Priya, the service owner, wants the precise target and rollback plan and fears acting on the wrong revision. Lena, the security lead, wants scoped authority and fears an instruction in a log becoming a command. These needs turn the architecture into testable work.

The acceptance contract requires a triage summary containing severity, blast radius and cited signals; an approval card containing exact arguments, evidence, impact, rollback, verification and expiry; a precondition mismatch that blocks mutation; factual communication derived from state; denial of injected authority; short-lived resource-bound execution credentials; a reviewable postmortem; and recorded per-incident usage. Each story should appear in a phase test or game day. Features with no story need a specific justification.

## Why this needs an agent

Investigation is adaptive: a trace finding can change which metric, runbook or deployment detail should be checked next. An agent helps generate and test hypotheses under bounded tools. It can prioritize independent investigations and explain uncertain evidence. The model should not determine accounting-like safety rules, authorizer roles, approval expiry or the permitted Kubernetes namespace. Those are stable rules better expressed as tested code.

An incident does not require nine agents by definition. The blueprint separates roles to teach the boundaries and support differing scopes; a smaller implementation can combine roles when the same permissions and context suffice. Keep a specialist only where it earns isolation, context, expertise or parallelism benefits. The capstone's teaching coverage must not become a requirement that every real deployment reproduce its number of agents.

## What the final demo must prove

Trigger a synthetic bad-deployment incident and show durable identity, triage and selected investigation. Gather metrics, logs, traces, changes and relevant runbooks through typed tools. Produce multiple plausible causes and tests that weaken alternatives. Present a bound rollback proposal, require the specified approval, execute with current preconditions and a stable effect key, then observe a complete recovery window. Show how the same system responds when one of those steps cannot establish its contract.

The final package also demonstrates a process restart, duplicate alert, injected log, stale approval and a successful command followed by unhealthy service metrics. It includes a timeline, evidence-to-action links and cost accounting. A single successful rollback is insufficient: the system must repeatedly preserve authority and tell the truth when it cannot resolve the incident. That is the behavior evaluated in Chapter 38.

## Non-functional requirements and SLOs

Use explicit illustrative targets to guide the capstone: triage p50 within three minutes and p95 within six; ranked hypotheses p50 within eight; no mutation without required authority; all report claims supported; complete approval cards; and no verified resolution without the specified observation evidence. Track model cost per SEV2 incident separately from infrastructure, with an illustrative median ceiling of two dollars and p95 of six. Replace these targets with local product requirements before live operation.

Ingestion and workflow availability have their own target, such as 99.9%, with humans still receiving alerts under degraded mode. Audit completeness and secret-free prompts are independent properties. An available agent can still propose poor actions, so uptime is not the headline outcome. Define sampling and denominators for each metric. Missing verification data is a failure to establish recovery, not a convenient absence of failing samples.

## System context and containers

The context view places AegisOps among responders, alerting, observability, knowledge, change management, Kubernetes, ITSM, chat, identity, models and partner agents. Distinguish read relationships from external writes and production changes. Restart, scale, feature-flag change and rollback all affect production and belong in the action boundary. ITSM and chat writes also need scoped authority and data-loss checks, even when they cannot restart a workload.

The deployable execution parts are an entry API, durable workflow coordinator, workers, approval service and scoped tool gateways or executors. Shared dependencies include identity, model gateway, policy, PostgreSQL, caches, object storage and telemetry. Some can be modules before becoming separate services. The C4 model distinguishes context and container views; a container view should describe actual deployment units rather than count every conceptual box as a container. Label protocols and owners at the boundaries that matter.

## Architecture

The entry API deduplicates transport events and correlates related alerts, then starts owned durable work. The incident workflow retains lifecycle state, waits and deadlines. The commander selects bounded investigations, while the harness enforces budgets, context and capability policy. The model gateway handles provider accounting. The evidence ledger stores source records independently of fluent explanations. The executor crosses the real change boundary; the verifier determines whether the goal was achieved.

This structure has two owners for different questions: the commander owns the incident plan, while deterministic services own authority and lifecycle invariants. A specialist cannot override the risk matrix through prose. A workflow replay does not repeat a recorded completed activity blindly, but a lost activity response can lead to retry. External effects therefore need downstream idempotency or safe reconciliation. The [Temporal activity contract](https://docs.temporal.io/activity-definition) explains that distinction.

## The agent team

Triage identifies affected service, severity and initial questions. Observability returns bounded metrics, logs and trace evidence. Knowledge retrieves versioned runbooks, ownership and past incidents. Change Intelligence examines releases, flags and infrastructure changes near the symptom onset. Security and Risk inspects signs of abuse and can narrow autonomy. Their outputs are structured findings with evidence, not independent instructions to other agents.

Remediation turns a supported hypothesis into a proposal with preconditions and verification; only the gated executor applies it. Communications produces state-derived updates with hypotheses labeled. Postmortem and Evaluator organize the timeline, lessons and quality evidence. The commander coordinates these roles and retains global budget ownership. Some roles need no model at all for a known task, and roles can share an implementation when that preserves their scope and accountability.

## Tool registry - minimum production tool set

Read tools include metric queries, bounded log search, trace retrieval, workload status, recent deployments, flag state, runbook search, past incidents and ownership lookup. Each accepts validated identifiers and limited windows, returns evidence and exposes typed failure. Scope comes from verified context; the model cannot choose another tenant through a free-form query. An authenticated source may supply reliable numeric observations while its text still has no instruction authority.

External-write tools create or update incident records and send templated updates. Production-change tools restart, scale, roll back and change feature flags. Each contract names risk, allowed resources, maximum blast radius, required preconditions, effect identity, uncertainty response and compensation. The `verify_slo` capability reads measurements and returns a deterministic criteria result. Its evidence references let later reviewers distinguish the change response from actual recovery.

## Composite skills

Package `diagnose_http_latency` as a bounded sequence of metrics, traces, dependency checks and change correlation. Package `diagnose_error_spike` around error slices, log signatures and known incidents. `assess_bad_deployment` compares versions and before/after evidence. These reusable procedures reduce repeated orchestration while retaining the exact evidence contract. A skill is useful because its steps and limits are tested, not because its name sounds like expertise.

`safe_restart` checks eligibility and replica safety, then uses the gated execution path and verification. `safe_rollback` includes revision preconditions, approval, rollout observation, recovery checks and a reviewed compensation route. `verify_recovery`, `incident_comms` and `generate_postmortem` each have distinct outputs. A procedural skill update changes behavior and therefore follows code review and evaluation. Do not let a postmortem model write a new production procedure into memory automatically.

## MCP and A2A boundaries

Group MCP servers by ownership and effect class: observability, knowledge, external records and production changes. Pin server identity and tool schemas; a read server that introduces a mutating capability fails discovery and execution policy. Discovery does not authorize use. MCP connects capabilities, while authorization remains layered in the caller, gateway and downstream service. Its current protocol details should follow the [official specification](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28).

Use A2A for a capability owned by another team, such as database reliability or security operations. The handoff includes task and correlation IDs, caller identity, allowed scope, deadline, evidence references and expected artifact shape. Validate the returned artifact and retain its provenance. A remote agent's confidence does not grant local mutation rights. Reattach to a known remote task after restart instead of creating duplicate delegated work.

## Canonical incident state

Store tenant, service, environment, trigger, detection time, severity and lifecycle status. Citation support comes from an evidence ledger whose entries retain immutable IDs, source/query locators, observation time and extracted facts. Hypotheses contain supporting and contradicting evidence, a possible falsification test and calibrated status labels. Proposed actions contain exact arguments, risk, preconditions, evidence, scope and recovery plan. Distinguish observed facts, inferences and authority rather than compressing all three into a `trusted` Boolean.

```python
from pydantic import BaseModel, Field
from typing import Literal

class ActionProposal(BaseModel):
    action_id: str
    incident_id: str
    tenant_id: str
    service_id: str
    environment: Literal["staging", "production"]
    operation: str
    args: dict
    args_hash: str
    risk: int = Field(ge=0, le=5)
    expected_revision: str
    evidence_ids: list[str] = Field(min_length=1)
    effect_id: str
    verification: dict
```

This schema is a proposal record, not an approval. The approval table references its action ID and hash, expiry, authorized distinct approvers and consumed or reserved status. The execution table references the same logical effect identity and stores pending, applied, rejected or unknown result. Unique local keys prevent duplicate intents, but they cannot make an external rollback atomic with the database. The adapter must enforce deduplication or reconciliation at the actual effect boundary.

## Memory design

Working memory is the current incident's durable state. Episodic memory stores reviewed previous incidents and successful or failed remediation sequences. Semantic knowledge includes curated runbooks, architecture and service records. Procedural memory is versioned skill code and configuration. Preferences can record low-risk team choices, such as update format. Each type needs provenance, retention and permitted use; none overrides current authority or system-of-record facts.

The write gate validates, classifies, redacts, deduplicates and obtains required review before persistence. A lesson such as “rollback solved this symptom” must retain the incident conditions and counterevidence, not become a universal command. Deletion covers serving records and derived indexes, with separately stated backup expiry and restore suppression. Recording lessons is useful only when the future investigator can see their scope and age.

## Approval and risk matrix

Risk 0 covers scoped evidence reads; risk 1 covers permitted templated external updates; risk 2 covers low-impact reversible non-production actions within bounds. Risk 3 covers production restart, bounded scaling and flag changes, requiring ordinary approval unless an explicit runbook permits the precise case. Risk 4 covers rollback and other broad or sensitive production changes, requiring privileged approval from two distinct authorized people. Risk 5 covers unknown, destructive or irreversible unsupported actions and is blocked for escalation.

The runbook exception applies to risk 3 only in this blueprint. It never admits risk 4 or 5 merely because a procedure exists. The service validates approvers, proposal identity, hash, expiry and consumption. Precondition re-validation checks current resource revision and business conditions at the mutation boundary. All capabilities need scoped credentials; production changes additionally need authority tied to the approved action. Short lifetime and audience binding reduce exposure without replacing resource checks.

## Threat model

Threat modelling includes prompt injection: logs, tickets, runbooks and remote artifacts can contain hostile instructions. Label them as data and constrain effect policy independently. Pin capability schemas and test schema drift against tool changes; deny unfamiliar tools rather than allowing a namespace prefix. Limit outgoing scopes, tenant data and blast radius. Bind approvals and prevent immediate replay before expiry. Enforce cumulative budgets against loops and costs. Use data-loss checks for communication and delegation, and a reviewed write gate for memory.

Map each threat to a negative test and residual risk. An injection suite proves behavior on its cases, not complete immunity. The model may still propose poor actions within its allowed space; evidence sufficiency, human review and independent verification address that remaining risk. Cross-tenant access should fail before data reaches context. Unknown outcomes should stop new effects until reconciliation. Threat modeling is most useful when it names the exact point that denies the adversarial request.

## The core state machine

Detailed lifecycle states include detected, triaged, investigating, hypothesis ready, plan ready, waiting approval, executing, verifying, resolved, escalated and failed safe. The state machine enforces required steps while models work inside investigation and planning. Rejection and expiry do not become execution. An unknown effect outcome enters reconciliation. Failed verification leads to bounded replanning or escalation with the failed action recorded as evidence.

## Technology stack

One implementation uses Python, FastAPI and Pydantic, with LangGraph behind owned interfaces and Temporal coordinating durable waits. Kafka carries alerts; PostgreSQL stores domain records and audit; Redis provides non-authoritative caches; object storage holds raw payloads; pgvector or a dedicated search system retrieves knowledge. OIDC, workload identity and policy-as-code govern access. OpenTelemetry, Prometheus and Grafana support operations. These are choices to justify, not mandatory brands.

Kubernetes and a reviewed deployment system host the services, with short-lived tool authority supplied outside model context. Git, Argo CD, flags, ITSM, paging and chat are adapters with their own owners. Use smaller model tiers for classification and bounded extraction, stronger tiers for hypothesis work when evaluations justify them, and an independently calibrated evaluator when needed. All model access goes through the LLM gateway for accounting and policy.

This is a deliberately smaller contract than the complete blueprint. Trusted local code supplies identity, one service, the risk class and evidence; no caller authentication, live model, MCP or A2A adapter is implied. A real Git service and local database cannot share that transaction: their integration must supply conditional ref update, stable operation lookup and reconciliation. ## Cost model

Estimate cost per incident from input and output tokens at each task tier. For illustration, small input/output cost is $0.25/$1.25 per million tokens, mid $3/$15 and strong $5/$25. These are example prices, not current provider quotes. A worked SEV2 run allocates triage 6k/0.6k tokens, investigation 140k/6k, hypotheses 60k/4k, planning 30k/2k, verification and communication 20k/2k, postmortem 40k/4k and sampled evaluation 30k/1k.

In that order, the selected tiers are small, mid, strong, strong, small, mid and mid. The corresponding model costs are $0.00225, $0.51, $0.40, $0.20, $0.0075, $0.18 and $0.105: $1.40475 in total. This excludes tool, storage, infrastructure and operating cost. Usage belongs in the incident record and postmortem, with estimates reconciled to billed provider usage. A slow human approval consumes elapsed time but not necessarily continuous model tokens; account for the implementation rather than assuming time and token cost are interchangeable.

## Reference repository structure

Organize applications around entry, workflow and worker deployments; keep agents, harness, tools, skills, MCP, A2A and retrieval as clear capability packages. Store policies, evaluation datasets and deterministic tests beside their owned contracts. Infrastructure, telemetry and architecture records have separate homes. This structure lets a reviewer find the authority rule without reading every agent prompt, and lets a replacement framework adapter preserve the business services.

The README should run a fake end-to-end incident before requiring real credentials. Pin dependencies and include commands for approval, crash and verification tests. A companion scaffold is a learning start, not evidence that every integration is production-ready. Publish the exact release/tag and test assumptions. Avoid empty folders that make the repository look complete while its critical effect and recovery mechanisms remain hidden TODOs.

## Failure modes

Property-based testing can vary approval identities, proposal revisions, expiry and crash timing while checking the same invariants: blocked actions never execute, altered proposals never inherit approval, and repeated transport attempts preserve one logical effect. Retain the generated counterexample as a deterministic regression set case. This complements model evaluations because it challenges the authority and recovery machinery without relying on fluent output.


A confident wrong diagnosis needs stronger competing evidence and verification, not broader authority. Specialist sprawl needs selection and budget measurements. Stale approval needs current preconditions enforced at mutation. An alert storm needs transport dedupe, correlation and admission control. Speculative communication needs state-derived claims. Silent model degradation needs versioned evaluation and rollout comparison. These controls make different failures distinguishable and repairable.

## Interview drills

Explain how a log instruction can influence a hypothesis without crossing the action gate. Trace one proposal from evidence to bound approval, scoped token, effect result and independent recovery check. For MCP versus A2A, identify whether the remote owner provides a tool contract or owns an autonomous task.

## Key takeaways

AegisOps earns adaptive reasoning in investigation and planning while keeping authority, lifecycle and completion criteria deterministic. One commander coordinates narrow roles; owned evidence and immutable proposals connect every decision to its basis. Risk policy bounds action, effect contracts support recovery, and a full observation window establishes resolution. The emergency-room analogy explains coordination, but the actual system becomes trustworthy through these inspectable engineering mechanisms.
